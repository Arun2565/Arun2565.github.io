What this section is
A daily log of things I learned — a paper I read, a bug I fixed, a concept I finally grokked. Short, dated, unfiltered. The goal is to learn in public and build a habit of reflection. Some entries are profound. Most are not. All of them are honest.
September 2026
Latent space isn't semantic space — it's usage-space
Sep 8
Embeddings cluster by how words are used, not what they mean. "Bank" (river) and "bank" (finance) have different embeddings not because the model understands the difference, but because they appear in different contexts. A paper finally made this click for me.
Source: "Geometric Structure of Neural Embeddings" — Arora et al.
Sentence-BERT cosine similarity breaks down past ~500 tokens
Sep 7
Long documents need chunking not just for context windows — the embeddings themselves degrade. Cosine similarity between a 1000-token passage and its 100-token summary was only 0.6. The embedding was averaging across too many topics.
Source: Testing with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 on PapersWithCode abstracts
Why chunk overlap matters more than chunk size for retrieval
Sep 6
Overlapping chunks by 20% gave better retrieval than doubling the chunk size. Context continuity at boundaries is more valuable than having more total chunks. A question at the edge of a chunk gets answered when the next chunk overlaps.
Source: PaperMind dev log — FAISS retrieval experiments
FAISS IndexIVFFlat: the cluster count tradeoff nobody explains
Sep 5
Too few clusters = slow search. Too many clusters = missed neighbors. The sweet spot is roughly sqrt(n) clusters for n vectors. But this assumes uniform distribution — real embeddings are clustered, so you need more clusters in high-density regions.
Source: FAISS documentation + empirical testing
Ollama's context window isn't what you think it is
Sep 4
Ollama reports 8192 context length for llama3.2:3b, but effective usable context is closer to 6000 tokens. Beyond that, the model starts ignoring earlier context. The "lost in the middle" problem is real and measurable.
Source: "Lost in the Middle" — Liu et al. 2023
LoRA rank isn't a hyperparameter — it's a budget
Sep 3
Rank 4 LoRA captures coarse patterns. Rank 16 captures details. Rank 64+ starts memorizing training data. Think of rank as "how many concepts can this adapter learn" rather than a tuning knob.
Source: LoRA paper + fine-tuning experiments
Gradio's file upload has a 2GB limit — and other things I learned the hard way
Sep 2
Building PaperMind's upload feature taught me more about browser limits than any documentation. 2GB file limit, no chunked upload by default, and memory spikes when processing large PDFs. Had to implement manual chunking.
Source: PaperMind dev log
Why I don't use vector databases for everything
Sep 1
ChromaDB, Qdrant, Pinecone — they're great for production. But for local development, FAISS flat index is faster, simpler, and has zero setup. Sometimes the boring choice is the right choice.
Source: Personal experience + benchmarks

The embedding model that changed how I think about retrieval
Aug 28
Switched from all-MiniLM-L6-v2 to bge-small-en-v1.5 for PaperMind. Same speed, 12% better retrieval on my test set. The difference? BGE models are trained specifically for retrieval, not just general embeddings.
Source: MTEB leaderboard + PaperMind experiments
RAG is not a database with a language model attached
Aug 25
RAG is a reasoning system that happens to use retrieval. The retrieval part is easy. The hard part is constructing prompts that make the model actually use the retrieved context instead of relying on its training data.
Source: "RAG is more than retrieval" — various papers
